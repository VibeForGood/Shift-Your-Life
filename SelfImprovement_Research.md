# Self Improvement Research

## Health outcomes and sustainable change

- In the Diabetes Prevention Program, people with prediabetes who aimed for
  about 7% weight loss and 150 minutes of activity per week reduced progression
  to type 2 diabetes by 58%, compared with 31% for metformin.
  - Knowler et al., *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2002.
- In the same programme, each kilogram of weight lost was associated with
  approximately 16% lower diabetes risk.
  - Hamman et al., *Diabetes Care*, 2006.
- Weight loss is associated with an average reduction of approximately 1 mmHg
  in blood pressure per kilogram lost.
  - Neter et al., *Hypertension*, 2003.
- Endurance exercise can reduce systolic blood pressure by approximately
  3.5 mmHg on average, with larger effects in people who start with higher
  blood pressure.
  - Cornelissen & Smart, *Journal of the American Heart Association*, 2013.
- A commonly recommended sustainable rate of weight loss is approximately
  0.5-1 kg per week. Very low energy intake can cause exhaustion, hunger, loss
  of muscle, and rebound eating.
  - NHS weight-loss guidance; CDC, "Losing Weight."
- Semaglutide and similar GLP-1 medications produced approximately 15% average
  weight loss over 68 weeks in a major trial. After treatment stopped, about
  two-thirds of the lost weight was regained within a year. Medication is a
  legitimate medical option to discuss with a clinician, not a moral failing.
  - Wilding et al., *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2021; Wilding et al.,
    *Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism*, 2022.
- Prediabetes is generally defined as HbA1c 5.7-6.4%. Stage 1 hypertension is
  generally at least 130/80 mmHg, and stage 2 is at least 140/90 mmHg.
  - American Diabetes Association Standards of Care; ACC/AHA 2017 guideline.

## Body weight and energy

- Resting energy can be estimated with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation. Daily
  needs also depend on movement, exercise, body size, age, and other individual
  factors.
  - Mifflin et al., *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, 1990.
- Approximately 7,700 kcal is often used as a rough estimate for the energy
  equivalent of one kilogram of tissue. This is not a fixed long-term rule:
  as body weight falls, energy expenditure changes and weight loss usually
  slows.
  - Hall et al., *The Lancet*, 2011.
- The old "3,500 kcal equals one pound forever" rule overestimates long-term
  weight loss because the body adapts.
- Day-to-day scale weight can vary by 1-2 kg because of water, salt, and stored
  carbohydrate. Each gram of glycogen is stored with roughly 3 g of water.
  - Kreitzman et al., *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, 1992.
- A rolling weekly average is more informative than a single daily weigh-in.
  Regular self-weighing is associated with better outcomes in behavioural
  weight-management programmes.
  - Zheng et al., *Obesity*, 2015; Burke et al., *Journal of the American
    Dietetic Association*, 2011.
- People who sleep less may eat approximately 385 kcal more the following day,
  without a corresponding increase in energy expenditure.
  - Al Khatib et al., *European Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, 2017.

## Habits and behaviour change

- In a study of people forming daily habits, automaticity took a median of
  66 days, with a range of 18-254 days. Progress followed an asymptotic curve:
  rapid early gains followed by a plateau. Missing one day did not meaningfully
  reset the habit.
  - Lally et al., *European Journal of Social Psychology*, 2010.
- The popular "21 days" claim came from a 1960 self-help book and was not a
  study of habit formation.
  - Maltz, *Psycho-Cybernetics*, 1960.
- Habits are learned links between a context and a response. Repeating a
  behaviour in the same time, place, or sequence helps the response become
  automatic.
  - Wood & Neal, *Psychological Review*, 2007; Wood & Runger, *Annual Review
    of Psychology*, 2016.
- An implementation intention is an if-then plan: "When situation X happens,
  I will do Y." Across 94 studies, implementation intentions had a medium to
  large effect on goal attainment (d = 0.65).
  - Gollwitzer & Sheeran, *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology*, 2006.
- Starting with a tiny behaviour and anchoring it to an existing routine can
  make repetition easier.
  - Fogg, *Tiny Habits*, 2019.
- A 23-laboratory replication found little evidence for the classic
  "ego-depletion" effect. People with strong self-control often encounter
  fewer temptations and rely on habits and environments rather than resisting
  temptations repeatedly.
  - Hagger et al., *Perspectives on Psychological Science*, 2016; Hofmann et
    al., *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2012; Galla &
    Duckworth, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2015.
- Replacing an old response when its cue appears is generally more practical
  than trying to erase the cue or rely on motivation alone.
  - Wood & Runger, *Annual Review of Psychology*, 2016.
- Self-monitoring of food, activity, and weight is one of the most consistent
  predictors of success in behavioural weight-management programmes.
  - Burke et al., *Journal of the American Dietetic Association*, 2011;
    Michie et al., *Health Psychology*, 2009.
- Temptation bundling, such as pairing exercise with an enjoyable activity,
  increased gym visits by up to 51% in an experimental study.
  - Milkman, Minson & Volpp, *Management Science*, 2014.
- The National Weight Control Registry has reported that many people who
  maintained substantial weight loss regularly ate breakfast, weighed
  themselves, limited television, and exercised. These are associations, not
  universal requirements.
  - Wing & Phelan, *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, 2005.

## Food and appetite

- In a month-long controlled inpatient trial, participants ate approximately
  500 kcal more per day on an ultra-processed diet than on a minimally
  processed diet and gained weight.
  - Hall et al., *Cell Metabolism*, 2019.
- Increasing dietary protein from 15% to 30% of calories led participants to
  eat approximately 441 kcal less per day without being instructed to reduce
  intake.
  - Weigle et al., *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, 2005.
- Protein, vegetables, and fibre can help meals remain filling for longer, but
  individual responses vary.
- Eating while distracted increases intake at that meal and later in the day.
  Eating attentively can reduce later snacking.
  - Robinson et al., *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, 2013.
- Food availability and visibility influence what people eat. Changing the
  home environment can be more reliable than relying on intentions.
  - Hollands et al., Cochrane Review, 2019; Wood & Neal, *Psychological
    Review*, 2007.
- Stress affects appetite differently across people. Those who eat more under
  stress often prefer sweet or high-fat comfort foods.
  - Oliver & Wardle, *Physiology & Behavior*, 1999; Oliver, Wardle & Gibson,
    *Psychosomatic Medicine*, 2000.
- After a dietary lapse, self-compassion may reduce the tendency to abandon
  the goal and continue overeating. The "what-the-hell effect" describes the
  opposite pattern of treating one lapse as total failure.
  - Herman & Mack, *Journal of Personality*, 1975; Adams & Leary, *Journal of
    Social and Clinical Psychology*, 2007.
- Evidence about non-sugar sweeteners and cravings is mixed. The World Health
  Organization advises against relying on them for long-term weight control.
  - WHO guideline on non-sugar sweeteners, 2023; Yang, *Yale Journal of
    Biology and Medicine*, 2010.
- Across 31 studies, sugar did not improve mood and was followed by greater
  tiredness and reduced alertness within about an hour.
  - Mantantzis et al., *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*, 2019.

## Urges and emotions

- Naming an emotion, known as affect labelling, can reduce activity in the
  amygdala and help people regulate difficult feelings.
  - Lieberman et al., *Psychological Science*, 2007; Torre & Lieberman,
    *Emotion Review*, 2018.
- Urges tend to rise, peak, and fall. Urge surfing means noticing the urge and
  allowing it to pass without immediately acting on it.
  - Bowen & Marlatt, *Psychology of Addictive Behaviors*, 2009.
- Five minutes of cyclic sighing improved mood and reduced physiological
  arousal in a controlled study.
  - Balban et al., *Cell Reports Medicine*, 2023.
- Cognitive reappraisal is generally more adaptive than suppressing emotional
  expression.
  - Gross & John, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2003.
- Expressive writing can help people process difficult experiences and
  organise thoughts.
  - Pennebaker, *Psychological Science*, 1997.
- Exercise is associated with reductions in depression and stress symptoms.
  - Noetel et al., *BMJ*, 2024.

## Sleep

- Adults generally need at least seven hours of sleep regularly.
  - Watson et al., *Sleep*, 2015; AASM and SRS consensus.
- Short sleep is associated with increased appetite, weight gain, diabetes,
  high blood pressure, depressed mood, and poorer judgement.
- Sleep loss increases responses to high-calorie food and reduces activity in
  brain regions involved in evaluating food choices.
  - Greer, Goldstein & Walker, *Nature Communications*, 2013.
- Caffeine has a half-life of approximately five hours. A dose taken six hours
  before bed can reduce sleep by more than an hour, even when people do not
  notice the effect.
  - Drake et al., *Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine*, 2013.
- Phone use in bed is associated with later sleep and poorer sleep quality.
  Engagement and continued interaction may matter more than blue light alone.
  - Exelmans & Van den Bulck, *Social Science & Medicine*, 2016; Kroese et
    al., *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2014.
- Alcohol can make it easier to fall asleep while disrupting the second half
  of the night and reducing REM sleep.
  - Ebrahim et al., *Alcoholism: Clinical and Experimental Research*, 2013.
- In habitual snoozers, up to 30 minutes of snoozing did not harm sleep or
  mood and slightly improved alertness. Its practical cost may instead be a
  rushed morning.
  - Sundelin et al., *Journal of Sleep Research*, 2023.

## Physical activity and fitness

- Adults are generally advised to complete 150-300 minutes of moderate
  activity or 75-150 minutes of vigorous activity per week, plus
  muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days.
  - Bull et al., *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 2020; WHO guidelines.
- Health benefits from daily steps tend to level off around 8,000-10,000
  steps for adults under 60, although there is no universal threshold.
  - Paluch et al., *The Lancet Public Health*, 2022.
- The 10,000-step target originated as a pedometer marketing slogan rather
  than a clinical limit.
- Exercise alone often causes modest weight loss, but it supports health and
  weight maintenance.
  - Swift et al., *Progress in Cardiovascular Diseases*, 2014.
- Strength training is associated with lower mortality risk.
  - Momma et al., *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 2022.
- The NHS Couch to 5K programme uses a gradual nine-week structure with three
  run sessions per week.

## Attention and productivity

- The mere presence of a smartphone can reduce available cognitive capacity,
  even when the phone is not being used.
  - Ward et al., *Journal of the Association for Consumer Research*, 2017.
- Interruptions and switching tasks create attention residue and can reduce
  focus on the next task. Batching notifications and email can reduce
  switching.
  - Leroy, *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*, 2009;
    Mark, Gudith & Klocke, CHI, 2008.
- Short breaks can improve freshness and reduce fatigue. Break quality varies;
  active or restorative breaks may be more helpful than passive scrolling.
  - Albulescu et al., *PLOS ONE*, 2022.

## Social connection

- Strong social relationships are associated with approximately 50% higher
  odds of survival across 148 studies.
  - Holt-Lunstad et al., *PLoS Medicine*, 2010.
- Friendship develops through time spent together: approximately 50 hours to
  become a casual friend, 90 hours to become a friend, and more than 200 hours
  to become a close friend.
  - Hall, *Journal of Social and Personal Relationships*, 2019.
- Responding positively to another person's attempts to connect is associated
  with stronger relationships.
  - Gottman & DeClaire, *The Relationship Cure*, 2001.
- Leisure, hobbies, and flow experiences are associated with better wellbeing.
  - Pressman et al., *Psychosomatic Medicine*, 2009; Csikszentmihalyi,
    *Flow*, 1990.
- Some findings about social contagion and relationship behaviour are
  observational and should not be treated as proof of direct causation.
  - Christakis & Fowler, *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2007.

## Limits and interpretation

- These findings describe averages and associations. They do not predict what
  will happen to one particular person.
- Weight, appetite, mood, sleep, and fitness are affected by medical
  conditions, medication, genetics, environment, and many other factors.
- Some simplified numbers are useful for explaining a concept but are not
  validated personal-health formulas, especially estimates for HbA1c,
  calories, and individual weight change.
- This document is for general research reference, not medical advice. Anyone
  concerned about weight, blood sugar, blood pressure, mood, eating, or sleep
  should speak with a qualified healthcare professional.
